{\LARGE\bfseries Shared-completion bounds for incomplete-benchmark decision stability}\par
\vspace{5pt}
{\large A technical contribution test, not an additional application}\par
22 September 2026\par
\end{center}

\textbf{Decision.} The investigation produces a substantive extension of the current
fixed-matrix analysis: sharp endpoints of winner-stability radii over an admissible
family of incomplete rank evidence. The lower endpoint decomposes over rivals;
the upper endpoint generally does not. Individually optimal pairwise completions
can be incompatible. An exact constructed example and an existing-evidence
experiment both exhibit this obstruction.

\textbf{No manuscript change.} CERT-Bench R3 remains frozen. The results below do
not replace its 122-task BeyondArena radius or its other benchmark results. This
note is a separate research artifact. Its proofs and computations were developed
within this project, not independently peer reviewed.

\section*{Main findings}
An exact three-task, three-model construction has 125 compatible weak-order
completions. Its sharp upper winner radius is $1/5$, while separately optimized
pairwise radii suggest $2/9$. Allowing new ties changes the sharp endpoint: it
would be $1/6$ if those ties were excluded.

The released-evidence experiment keeps all 122 complete 11-model rank rows and
the nine-model relative orders on 20 additional BeyondArena tasks. On this
\emph{declared coarsened ordinal evidence}, the following endpoints are sharp:
\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{lccc}\toprule
Fixed candidate & Lower endpoint & Sharp upper & Pairwise upper relaxation\\\midrule
TabPFN-2.6 & $0$ & $96/1207=0.07953604$ & $96/1207$\\
RealMLP & $0$ & $55/2414=0.02278376$ & $69/2698=0.02557450$\\
Other nine models & $0$ & $0$ & $0$\\\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}
For RealMLP, the relaxation overstates the upper endpoint by
$64/22933=0.00279074$, or $128/1045=12.2488\%$ relative to the sharp endpoint.
A compatible rank completion attains $55/2414$. An independent integer dynamic
program proves that no compatible completion exceeds it; this conclusion does
not depend on a floating-point mixed-integer solver's optimality flag.

\textbf{Evidence restriction.} The 40 unspecified positions in the selected
representation are not asserted to be the original raw missing-cell pattern.
Exact raw score/failure records were not recovered. Additional observed scores,
metric bounds or failure semantics could narrow the completion family. The
endpoint matrices are hypothetical proof witnesses, not new evaluations or
imputed estimates. These are sharp endpoints for the declared ordinal family,
not claimed sharp endpoints for the unrecovered full raw record.

\textbf{Novelty restriction.} Necessary/possible winners, incomplete-rank
aggregation, TV optimization and ILP are established foundations
\cite{himmi,xia,chakraborty,tv}. The candidate contribution is their specific
quantitative extension to sharp, rank-consistent task-weight stability envelopes,
with a verified shared-completion obstruction. A first-in-literature claim is
not established by the present search.

\section{Fixed scope, incomplete evidence, and sharp endpoints}
There are $D$ tasks and $M$ models. For each task $d$, let the nonempty finite set
$\Pset_d\subset[0,1]^M$ contain every admissible full normalized rank-loss row.
Ranks are lower-is-better, use average positions for ties, and are normalized by
$M-1$. Observed order and equality relations are preserved. Unspecified models
are inserted into a full weak order, not assigned independent rank intervals.
A shared completion is $Z\in\C=\prod_{d=1}^D\Pset_d$.

Task scope, candidate scope, and nominal weights $w^0\in\Delta_D$ are held fixed
throughout, where $\Delta_D=\{w\in\R^D:w\geq0,\ \sum_d w_d=1\}$.
For gap vector $g$, define the established TV boundary problem
\begin{equation}
\Phi(g)=\inf\{\tfrac12\lVert w-w^0\rVert_1:w\in\Delta_D,\ w^\top g\leq0\}.
\end{equation}
Its value is $+\infty$ if the feasible set is empty and $0$ if the nominal gap
is already nonpositive. For a fixed candidate $i$,
\begin{equation}
\rho_i(Z)=\min_{j\ne i}\Phi(g^{ij}(Z)),\qquad
 g_d^{ij}(Z)=Z_{dj}-Z_{di}.
\end{equation}
The sharp completion endpoints are
\begin{equation}
\rlo_i=\min_{Z\in\C}\rho_i(Z),\qquad
\rhi_i=\max_{Z\in\C}\rho_i(Z).
\end{equation}
The finite completion family ensures attainment, allowing $+\infty$. The bracket
$[\rlo_i,\rhi_i]$ is an envelope: it need not equal the set of attainable radii.
It is neither a confidence interval nor a probability model for missing scores.

\begin{proposition}[Sharp lower endpoint and pairwise upper relaxation]
For $j\ne i$, define
$\ell_d^j=\min_{p\in\Pset_d}(p_j-p_i)$ and
$u_d^j=\max_{p\in\Pset_d}(p_j-p_i)$. Then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:bounds}
\rlo_i=\min_{j\ne i}\Phi(\ell^j),\qquad
\rhi_i\leq U_i^{\rm pair}:=\min_{j\ne i}\Phi(u^j).
\end{equation}
Each $\Phi(u^j)$ is the sharp upper \emph{pairwise} endpoint. Equality in the
second statement is certified by a single admissible completion with radius
$U_i^{\rm pair}$; separate pairwise witnesses do not suffice.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
If $g\leq h$ componentwise, nonnegative task weights give
$\{w:w^\top h\leq0\}\subseteq\{w:w^\top g\leq0\}$; hence
$\Phi(g)\leq\Phi(h)$. Row independence makes each gap vector $\ell^j$ and $u^j$
attainable for its particular rival. Interchanging the two finite minima gives
$\min_Z\min_j\Phi(g^{ij}(Z))=\min_j\Phi(\ell^j)$. Conversely,
$\max_Z\min_j\Phi(g^{ij}(Z))\leq\min_j\max_Z\Phi(g^{ij}(Z))
=\min_j\Phi(u^j)$. The last maxima can use different completions. A shared
witness attaining the upper bound proves equality.
\end{proof}
\begin{corollary}[Evidence tightening]
If consistent additional evidence gives a nonempty family $\mathcal F\subseteq\C$,
then $\rlo_i(\C)\leq\rlo_i(\mathcal F)\leq\rhi_i(\mathcal F)\leq\rhi_i(\C)$.
Thus a coarse-family upper bound remains an outer bound after refinement, but
its endpoint witness may cease to be admissible. This follows by set inclusion.
\end{corollary}

\section{A strict shared-completion obstruction, including ties}
Consider three equally weighted tasks and models $A,B,C$. The observed loss
rows are
\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{lccc}\toprule
Task & $A$ & $B$ & $C$\\\midrule
1 & 2 & 0 & unspecified\\
2 & 1 & unspecified & 0\\
3 & 1 & unspecified & 2\\\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}
Only the shown order relations are imposed. The unspecified value can be below,
between, above, or tied with either of the two distinct observed values. There
are five weak orders per row and $5^3=125$ shared completions.

In task 1, placing $C$ above both observed losses, so $B<A<C$, maximizes both
rival-minus-$A$ gaps componentwise. In task 2, placing $B$ above both observed
losses, so $C<A<B$, does the same. These dominance replacements cannot decrease
$A$'s stability under any nonnegative weights. On task 3, only three nondominated
weak orders remain:
\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{lccc}\toprule
Task 3 order & $(g^B_3,g^C_3)$ & $\rho_A$ & Role\\\midrule
$A<B<C$ & $(1/2,1)$ & $1/6$ & Best for gap to $C$\\
$A<(B=C)$ & $(3/4,3/4)$ & $1/5$ & Joint optimum\\
$A<C<B$ & $(1,1/2)$ & $1/6$ & Best for gap to $B$\\\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}
For the joint optimum the gap vectors are
\begin{equation}
g^B=(-1/2,1/2,3/4),\qquad g^C=(1/2,-1/2,3/4).
\end{equation}
Each nominal gap is $1/4$. Moving weight from the largest gap $3/4$ to the
smallest gap $-1/2$ reduces the aggregate gap by $5/4$ per unit moved. The required
TV movement is $(1/4)/(5/4)=1/5$, below the donor's available mass $1/3$.
This proves $\rhi_A=1/5$ after the dominance reductions.

In contrast, the coordinatewise maximal pairwise vectors are
$u^B=(-1/2,1/2,1)$ and $u^C=(1/2,-1/2,1)$. Their boundary radii are
$(1/3)/(3/2)=2/9$. The required last positions of $B$ and $C$ on task 3 conflict:
no single row can have both gaps equal to 1. Therefore
\begin{equation}
\rhi_A=\frac15 < \frac29=U_A^{\rm pair}.
\end{equation}
Direct rational enumeration gives exactly the attainable set
$\{0,1/15,1/6,1/5\}$, independently checked with fresh primal LPs. In particular,
the envelope $[0,1/5]$ does not imply all intermediate radii are attainable.
Disallowing new ties removes the middle joint-optimum row and gives upper
endpoint $1/6$.

\textbf{Interpretation.} The obstruction is not that a challenger reaches a
nominal winner before becoming globally feasible, as in R3. Here the issue is
that the strongest evidence completion against one rival is inconsistent with
that against another. The completion is required to be shared across every
rival and every admissible reweighting.

\section{An exact fixed-budget formulation}
Let $B_\epsilon=\{w\in\Delta_D:\TV(w,w^0)\leq\epsilon\}$ and define
\begin{equation}\label{eq:eta}
\eta_i^*(\epsilon)=\max_{Z\in\C}\min_{j\ne i}\min_{w\in B_\epsilon}
 w^\top g^{ij}(Z).
\end{equation}
\begin{proposition}[One completion, all rivals, all permitted weights]
$\eta_i^*(\epsilon)>0$ if and only if some compatible completion has
$\rho_i(Z)>\epsilon$. For explicitly enumerated row patterns, the value in
\eqref{eq:eta} is the optimum of a mixed-integer linear program.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
The TV ball is compact. Absence of a nonpositive pairwise margin on that ball is
therefore equivalent to a strictly positive minimum margin against every rival.
A finite maximum over completions preserves the existence statement. Weak
nonnegativity is insufficient at a tie.

For fixed $g$, the dual of its TV-ball support minimization is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:dual}
\max_{\nu,t,v}\ \nu+(w^0)^\top v-2\epsilon t
\quad\text{s.t.}\quad \nu+v_d\leq g_d,\quad -t\leq v_d\leq t,\quad t\geq0.
\end{equation}
This follows by introducing absolute-deviation variables in the primal and
using LP duality. Both primal feasibility and finite optimality hold.
Index row patterns by $p$. Introduce $y_{dp}\in\{0,1\}$ with
$\sum_p y_{dp}=1$. For each rival $j$, use its own $(\nu_j,t_j,v^j)$, with the
same $y$ across all rivals. Maximize $\eta$ subject to
\begin{align}
\nu_j+v^j_d&\leq\sum_{p\in\Pset_d}y_{dp}(p_j-p_i),\\
-t_j&\leq v^j_d\leq t_j,\qquad t_j\geq0,\\
\eta&\leq\nu_j+\sum_d w_d^0v^j_d-2\epsilon t_j\qquad(j\ne i).
\end{align}
Strong duality establishes equality with \eqref{eq:eta}. There are no products
between continuous weight variables and binary completion variables. Relaxing
$y$ to mixtures is only a relaxation: a mixture need not be a full rank row.
\end{proof}

The program is exact as a mathematical formulation. Floating-point MILP output
still requires tolerance checks and does not itself prove an exact endpoint.
No general polynomial-time guarantee is claimed; possible-winner problems and
practical ILP precedents already exist for partial rankings and partial chains
\cite{chakraborty,chains}.

\textbf{Bounded implementation.} Since $g_d\in[-1,1]$, a dual optimum can be
chosen with $t\leq1$, $|\nu|\leq1$ and $|v_d|\leq1$. To see this, put
$m=\min_d g_d$. For a cutoff $c\in[m,\max_d g_d]$, take
$t=(c-m)/2$, $\nu=(c+m)/2$, and $v_d=\min(g_d,c)-\nu$. These realize
\begin{equation}\label{eq:clipgeneral}
\min_{w\in B_\epsilon}w^\top g
=\max_{c\in[m,\max g]}\left\{\sum_d w_d^0\min(g_d,c)-\epsilon(c-m)\right\}.
\end{equation}
For any feasible dual, setting $c=\nu+t$ shows its objective is bounded above by
this expression, or by the endpoint $c=m$ if $c<m$; cutoffs beyond $\max g$ do not
improve it. This also proves the stated bounds. TV extrema are an established
foundation, not a separate novelty claim \cite{tv}.

\section{Exact upper certification without trusting a solver flag}
For the uniform 142-task experiment, all full normalized average ranks are on
the grid $\{0,1/20,\ldots,1\}$. Scale a rival gap by 20 to get integers $a_d$,
and let $m=\min_d a_d$. Equation~\eqref{eq:clipgeneral} gives the exact identity
\begin{equation}\label{eq:clipint}
\min_{w\in B_\epsilon}\sum_d w_d\frac{a_d}{20}
=\frac{1}{20D}\max_{v\in\{m,\ldots,\max a\}}
\left\{\sum_d\min(a_d,v)-D\epsilon(v-m)\right\}.
\end{equation}
The expression is piecewise linear between integer gaps, so a maximizing cutoff
can be chosen on the integer grid. For $D\epsilon=k+h$, $k$ integer and
$0<h<1$, the $(k+1)$st largest gap is a maximizing cutoff: the slope changes
from positive to negative there. Equivalently, remove the top $k$ gaps and a
fraction $h$ of the next gap, moving that weight to a minimum-gap row.

\subsection*{The RealMLP certificate}
Set $\epsilon=55/2414$ so that $D\epsilon=55/17=3+4/17$. Use two rivals,
$A=$TabICLv2 and $B=$TabPFN-2.6. Across the 122 complete rows, their integer gap
sums are $-126$ and $-208$; their minima are $m_A=-16$ and $m_B=-18$.
Every admissible pattern on the 20 partial rows has gaps no smaller than these
minima. Thus these minima are fixed across all completions.

A maximizing cutoff is the fourth largest full gap. Known complete rows imply
$v_A\in\{18,19,20\}$ and $v_B\in\{14,15,\ldots,20\}$. Only 21 cutoff pairs need
checking, while retaining \emph{every} compatible joint gap pair on each partial
row. Let $K_A(v_A)$ and $K_B(v_B)$ be the corresponding sums of clipped known
gaps. For a partial row $r$, let $Q_r$ be its possible pairs $(a,b)$ of integer
gaps to the two rivals.

For a fixed cutoff pair, define $F_r(s)$ as the largest accumulated clipped
$B$-gap over the first $r$ partial rows with accumulated clipped $A$-gap equal to
$s$. Initialize $F_0(0)=0$ and other states $-\infty$. The exact recurrence is
\begin{equation}
F_r(s)=\max_{(a,b)\in Q_r}\left\{
 F_{r-1}\bigl(s-\min(a,v_A)\bigr)+\min(b,v_B)\right\}.
\end{equation}
For the first rival to have a strictly positive support margin,
\begin{equation}
s> \frac{55}{17}(v_A-m_A)-K_A(v_A).
\end{equation}
Since $s$ is integer, the lower state threshold is the floor of the right-hand
side plus 1. For all such states, the second rival's scaled support residual is
at most
\begin{equation}
K_B(v_B)+\max_{s\text{ above threshold}}F_{20}(s)
-\frac{55}{17}(v_B-m_B).
\end{equation}
Exact integer/rational evaluation across all 21 cutoff pairs gives maximum
$-33/17$, strictly negative (normalized margin $-33/48280$). No completion can
make both rival margins positive at this radius. Hence $\rhi_{\rm RealMLP}\leq
55/2414$. The stored shared-completion witness has exact minimum pairwise
radius $55/2414$, proving equality. The certificate uses necessary conditions
from two rivals to exclude \emph{all} completions; it is not an enumeration of
$381^{20}$ complete matrices.

\section{Existing-evidence experiment and its limits}
The input files are the byte-identical author-derived matrices
\path{primary11__all__rank__equal__normalized_matrix.csv} and
\path{coverage9__all__rank__equal__normalized_matrix.csv}
from the R3 supplementary package. Their dimensions are $122\times11$ and
$142\times9$. Restricting the complete matrix to the nine common models and
recomputing ranks reproduces the bridge's 122 common rows with zero discrepancy.

On the 20 remaining tasks, only the nine-model relative order is retained;
TabDPT and TabPFN-2.6 positions are unspecified. Inserting the two models,
including possible ties, gives 381 full rank rows per task. The completion family
therefore has $381^{20}\approx4.1543\times10^{51}$ matrices and preserves 7,430
observed unordered-pair relations. No distribution over those matrices is used.

\textbf{TabPFN-2.6.} The sharp lower endpoint is zero: a valid lower witness
makes RealMLP the unique nominal winner. A single upper witness attains the
pairwise upper relaxation $96/1207$. Proposition~1 therefore certifies the sharp
envelope $[0,96/1207]$.

\textbf{RealMLP.} Its sharp lower endpoint is also zero. Separate pairwise upper
optimization gives $69/2698$, but no shared completion attains that radius.
The shared endpoint witness and Section~4's exact obstruction give
$[0,55/2414]$. Its endpoint witness has the following exact boundary radii:
\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{lrlr}\toprule
Rival & Radius & Rival & Radius\\\midrule
LinearModel & $319/923$ & XGBoost & $43/284$\\
RandomForest & $79/284$ & TabM & $137/1420$\\
ExtraTrees & $963/4544$ & TabDPT & $669/4544$\\
CatBoost & $301/3408$ & TabPFN-2.6 & $31/1349$\\
LightGBM & $91/710$ & TabICLv2 & $55/2414$\\\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}
For each of the other nine models, a zero pairwise upper relaxation proves that
it cannot be a unique nominal winner under any completion. This does not rule
out co-winner status by an independent argument and is not presented as such.

\textbf{What this does not show.} The original complete-case result
$41/2196\approx0.01867031$ for TabPFN-2.6 uses 122 equal task weights. The new
endpoints use 142 equal task weights and a different evidence family. They do
not correct, contradict, or improve that original number. No result is a
probability of reversal, a deployment recommendation, or a universal robustness
scale. No new result is asserted for top-$k$ or global feasible-winner entry.

The inherited retrieval manifest records original raw hashes and byte counts,
but not exact download URLs. Neither recovered archive supplied the original
raw score/failure mask. G1 thus deliberately coarsens the released evidence.
It does not assert that all 40 unspecified positions were originally missing,
that unsupported evaluations have ordinary latent scores, or that every ordinal
completion is compatible with unrecovered metric bounds. Those distinctions
must be resolved before a sharp \emph{original-raw} missingness claim.

\subsection*{What can credibly be claimed}
The exact object differs from obtaining a point ranking by completing missing
scores \cite{himmi} or estimating identifiable scores from incomplete samples
\cite{onebench}. It quantitatively refines possible/necessary unique-winner
analysis \cite{xia,chakraborty} by retaining a continuum of TV task-weight
perturbations. Its mathematical foundations remain familiar: minimax ordering,
TV support duality, partial rankings, and discrete optimization
\cite{tv,chains}. The present search does not establish priority for the complete
combination or an absence of equivalent earlier formulations.

The contribution candidate is therefore specific: \emph{sharp completion
endpoints for task-reweighting stability, a proof that sharp pairwise endpoints
can overstate joint winner stability, and checkable shared-completion endpoint
certificates}. The real-data relaxation gap demonstrates that respecting one
shared completion changes the answer, rather than merely adding computation.

\subsection*{Next decision}
G1 is a mathematical/computational pass for its declared ordinal model. Keep R3
as the active manuscript. Before integration, complete the closest-prior-work
comparison and choose whether to report the precisely labeled ordinal-evidence
experiment or tighten it using the original raw mask and metric/failure
constraints. The latter requires exact artifact recovery, not a different
benchmark or a newer living-registry snapshot. Added consistent constraints
must narrow the envelope; endpoint values must not be protected by omitting
newly recovered evidence. Integration should replace repetitive exposition,
not append another application or inflate dataset counts.
